Rick has all his Chips
Well, it’s not very often you see a green arrow of 4.7 million, eh? The same team that languished 4 points behind the average in GW1 immediately shrugged off the funk and delivered 36 points above average in GW2. I was expecting the team to come good, but I didn’t think it would swing so quickly. I’ll happily take it, though.
It certainly goes to show how volatile things can be at the start of the season. It was needed, though, if only for the psychology of things. However, it’s worth remembering that it’s just as easy to fall back down again. A couple of points make a huge difference when ranks are packed this tightly. Complacency isn’t an option and there’s still a very long way to go.
I’m now sat at a far more comfortable rank of 845k, which is pretty respectable considering roughly 12% of players have already used Bench Boost while I still have mine to come. Let’s hope things stay on the up.
The Plan
My original plan, and the whole reason I kept 0.5m in the bank from the start, was to switch Mbeumo to Semenyo this week, giving me the supposedly ultimate Man City premium triple up of Haaland, Semenyo and O’Reilly against Coventry at home. My main concern was whether I’d get to GW3 without a price shift scuppering that plan. I got lucky. No scuppering. But it turns out there are other things to consider…
Firstly, it’s the emergence of Cherki as the FPL favourite Man City mid. He’s not only more in-form than Semenyo, but also now a greater threat to my rank. He’s cheaper, though, so even after his multiple price rises he’s still a viable option should I want to swap Mbeumo to him instead. It spits in the eye of my original plan, but it’s a safer route if I’m worried about swimming against the tide. I am a bit concerned about his minutes though…
The other consideration is Tzolis. I knew he was a game-time risk going in, but after his assist in GW1 I still thought he was a savvy pick. One week later and it’s a different story. Scoring zero and being subbed at 45 mins in GW2 has wrecked my confidence in him. The markets won’t take kindly to that, and it’s Chelsea up next, so I’m sure I’ll start losing money on him soon.
I’m not a fan of knee-jerk decisions (and there’s every chance he’ll start against Chelsea) but, frankly, choosing to drop Mbeumo after he’s just scored 11 points (and faces Everton next) instead of Tzolis feels questionable right now.
The problem with doing that is, unlike Mbeumo, I can’t afford to switch Tzolis to either of my preferred Man City mids with a single FT. Both Semenyo and Cherki are too expensive without using an additional transfer to downgrade elsewhere – something I’m really not keen on, as I don’t want to diminish the team any further than I have to and I have plans that require two transfers next week.
That in mind, it would have to be Anderson or Foden if I opted to switch out Tzolis. Of the two I’d prefer Foden, who I currently have the exact money for thanks to that saved 0.5m. He’s not scored in a long time, but he’s looking lively this season, and might be worth punting on for a couple of weeks before my planned WC in GW6.

Hello FFGeek patrons, and welcome to my GW3 write-up. It was a pretty crazy few days of FPL over the Bank Holiday weekend with points flying around, and I feel fortunate to have come out the other side with a reasonably positive outcome. We’ll choose to ignore the Pascal Gross haul on the bench and instead focus on already registering a 100+ gameweek score, primarily thanks to the huge haul from captain Bruno. The value of defcons was also on display as all of my outfield players scored at least four points, with the likes of Muharemovic and Szoboszlai picking up the additional two points. Plenty of success in the opening couple of weeks, however the cloud of the Ndiaye and Sarr transfer sagas remains as we head towards GW3.
Squad Assessment
Before we come onto that pair, let’s take a look elsewhere. An interesting stat for my team is that 12 of my 15 players have already registered some form of return in the opening two weeks. The exceptions are Sarr (yet to feature), Diop (a 4.0mil defender) and Kinsky (the less said about Spurs, the better). It is a simple measurement, but certainly gives me reason for confidence in the current squad.
Despite the lack of returns from Kinsky, I’m content with my goalkeepers and see no reason to make a change, with some decent fixtures between him and Verbruggen over the medium term. In attack, all three forwards scored in GW2 and, although alternatives to Calvert-Lewin in the cheap striker slot are beginning to present themselves, there is no need to use a transfer here in the short term.
I do have slightly more concerns in defence. Calafiori and Gvardiol have both registered attacking returns in their respective fixtures, helping them to outscore their more expensive teammates. This is unlikely to continue and I do have reservations about how secure their places will be as we move through the season. Regardless, I see them as strong assets until they show themselves to be otherwise. In simple terms, I’m happy to stick with them and enjoy the returns until one drops out of the starting XI, at which point I can reassess. Muharemovic is looking like the defcon pick I hoped he would be, while Leeds have conceded just once so far to continue their solid defensive form. Maguire is the one who could become a priority transfer, with limited defcon potential in a vulnerable-looking defence and plenty of good alternatives reachable around his price point.
Midfield is obviously the interesting area. Szoboszlai and Gross have already shown themselves to be strong all-round FPL assets at their respective prices, so I’m very happy to own both. Fernandes also demonstrated his potential power as a captaincy pick on Sunday, although he may not come into consideration again until GW6. I can therefore understand why some managers may consider downgrading him now or in GW4, although I’m in no rush to do so.
I spoke in detail about Sarr & Ndiaye in my previous article, where my conclusion was to bide my time and wait for more clarity on their situations. I do have some clarity, but still not nearly enough to make confident decisions. Ndiaye has joined Man City, which is clearly an upgrade from a team perspective, but his minutes are highly uncertain and he certainly won’t have penalties anymore. On the other hand, Sarr has remained at Crystal Palace and I now await news on his ‘injury’ to see whether he immediately slots back into the team. Hopefully more information presents itself before the weekend.
